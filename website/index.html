Two studies, two tools

Each starts with a question we could be wrong about, and ends with something a person can read, check or use.

  1. Study 1 · Done

    Does knowing what a mouse is doing help predict its visual cortex?

    A model that sees only the picture, against one that also knows pupil size and running speed. Three mice, one held-out test.

    Result: better in all three mice, by +0.032 to +0.049. Open the study →

  2. Study 2 · Done

    Can brain waves tell when eyes are closed?

    One person, 14 EEG channels, 117 seconds. Always trained on the past and tested on the future.

    Result: 0.625 on the last block (0.728 without four glitches), but near a coin flip across time. Open the study →

    At Pragnya’s Sunday outings, “help out” is not a job, and spoken instructions are hard to hold on to.

    Helper Jobs: a real job in picture steps, with a volunteer close by. See the tools →

Under all of it, honesty: results are kept even when they disappoint, and every number on these pages is generated from committed results. Three mice and one EEG recording cannot speak for all mice or all people.

What this can’t tell us yet: whether these patterns hold in more animals and more people, or what causes them. Next: five experiments with the same steps.

How we tried to break each result

A result is only shared after a check that could have made it disappear.

  • Shuffle the answers

    A coin flip, as it should be

    With the EEG labels scrambled 20 times, the score falls to 0.499. Mouse behaviour shuffled 20 times adds almost nothing.

  • Split by time

    Train on the past, test the future

    No random mixing of neighbouring moments, which makes EEG results look far better than they are.

  • Leave a gap

    90 samples between train and test

    Removing the moments right next to each test block keeps the EEG result near chance: it is not leakage.

  • Leave one out

    No single mouse carries it

    The average gain stays positive (+0.040 to +0.048) whichever mouse is left out.

  • Glitch check

    Four broken samples found

    Values up to 715,897 where normal is about 4,000. Limiting them helps the last block, not the drift.

  • Every number traced

    Rebuilt from saved results

    The charts are generated from the committed results files, and the EEG study reruns with one command.

Who did what

The studies were done by the applicant with a parent collaborator, who helped scope questions, review code and debug. Software tools, including AI assistants, helped build this website. The contribution ledger lists each part.

What it is not

It is not a clinical or diagnostic study. Three mice and one EEG recording cannot speak for all animals or people, and a model that predicts something does not show what causes it.
