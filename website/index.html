From neurons to next steps

At Pragnya’s outings for neurodivergent kids and adults, and in everyday schoolwork, the same instruction can land one moment and be lost the next. That raised a question: does what a brain is doing (its state: alert, tired, calm) change what it takes in? We tested it in two public brain recordings, tried hard to break each answer, and turned what held up into the design of two apps.

One loop, five chapters

It starts in real life and ends back there. Each chapter answers the question the last one raised.

  1. 1 · Real life

    Same words, different moment.

    At Pragnya’s outings and in schoolwork, a step that is easy when someone is calm can be lost when they are tired, rushed or overloaded. Is that the brain itself?

    To find out: look inside a brain while its state changes.

  2. 2 · Understand

    A mouse’s state changes how its brain sees.

    Mice watched pictures while thousands of brain cells were recorded. We guessed what each cell would do from the picture alone, then from the picture plus how alert and active the mouse was.

    Found: knowing the mouse’s state made our guesses 25–33% better, in all three mice. Read chapter 2 →

  3. 3 · Measure

    Can sensors read state in a person?

    A cap of 14 sensors on one person’s head for two minutes (EEG). Closing your eyes is one of the clearest changes it picks up. Can a simple model tell when?

    Found: on one stretch, often (0.625, where 0.5 is a coin flip). Across the whole recording it drifted to 0.478. Brain-wave bands did better (0.566) but still dipped below guessing. Read chapter 3 →

  4. 4 · Design

    From findings to features.

    If state matters but cannot be read from outside, an app should ask, not guess. It should show less on a low-energy day, and make pausing and coming back easy.

    Built into: Next Step, by Amishi Mehta, for schoolwork, and Helper Jobs, a family project, built for real jobs at Pragnya’s Sunday outings. Read chapter 4 →

  5. 5 · Test, then go further

    Where this goes next.

    One follow-up done, three more data experiments, design studies with Pragnya volunteers and families, and papers others can check.

    The aim: one research direction, where neuroscience and design meet. Read chapter 5 →

The loop: real life raised the question; the mouse showed that state changes what a brain takes in; the EEG showed that state is hard to read from outside; so the apps ask how someone is doing and keep each step small. The last experiment takes the apps back to real life to see if they help. Three mice and one person are a start, not a rule for everyone.

What this does not show yet: whether the pattern holds in more animals and more people, what causes it, or whether the apps help. Each of those is an experiment in chapter 5.

How we tried to break each result

Nothing in the story counts until it has survived a check that could have made it disappear.

  • Mix up the answers

    It should fail, and it does

    If we scramble which moments were eyes-closed, the model drops to a coin flip. If we scramble the mouse’s state, the gain disappears. So the results come from real signal.

  • Never peek at the future

    Learn from earlier, test on later

    The model only ever learns from the past and is tested on what comes after, the way a real app would have to work.

  • Leave a gap

    No help from next-door moments

    Moments right next to each other look alike. We leave a gap between what the model learns from and what it is tested on.

  • Leave one mouse out

    No single mouse carries it

    Take any one mouse away and the other two still show the gain.

  • Look for broken readings

    Four found and capped

    Four readings were up to 180 times bigger than normal. Capping them helps one test, but does not fix the drift.

  • Every number traced

    Rebuilt from saved results

    Every chart is drawn from the saved results files, and the EEG study reruns with one command.

Who did what

The studies were done by Amishi with her father, who helped shape the questions, review the code and fix problems. Software tools, including AI assistants, helped build this website and the apps. The contribution ledger lists each part.

What it is not

It is not a medical study and it does not diagnose anyone. Three mice and one person cannot speak for everyone, and being able to predict something is not the same as knowing what causes it.
