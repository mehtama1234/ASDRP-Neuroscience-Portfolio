Chapter 3 · From the outside · EEG study

Can a sensor on the head tell when someone’s eyes are closed?

Eyes closed is one of the clearest changes in brain waves. If a simple model cannot read it reliably over two minutes, quieter states like tiredness will be harder still.

On one stretch, yes. Over time, no. The model scored 0.625 on the last part of the recording, and 0.728 once four glitch samples were limited. Tested block by block across the recording, it averaged 0.478, about a coin flip. Giving it the last few moments of signal did not help.

How we tested it

Always train on the past, and test on the future.

  • The recording

    One person, 117 seconds

    14 channels and 14,980 samples, about 45% with eyes closed.

  • Split by time

    70 · 15 · 15

    Learn on the first 70%, choose on the next 15%, test once on the last 15%. No random mixing.

  • Two models

    A moment, or a moment plus history

    One sees a single moment; the other also sees the average and spread of the last 9 samples.

  • The score

    ROC AUC

    0.5 is a coin flip and 1.0 is perfect.

  • Five blocks

    Forward in time

    Train on everything before a block, test on the block, five times.

  • The data

    UCI EEG Eye State

    Roesler (2013), a public recording with each moment marked eyes open or closed.

The results

Final test block

Good on one block, better without glitches.

Four samples are recording glitches, with values up to 715,897 where normal readings are about 4,000. Limiting each channel to the range seen in training lifts the simple model from 0.625 to 0.728.

The glitch check was added after the first results, so it is shown next to the original numbers, not instead of them.

Five blocks in time

Over time, it drifts to a coin flip.

Scores scatter around 0.5, and several blocks fall well below it. A model that is reliably worse than chance has learned a pattern that flips later in the recording.

That points to drift across the 117 seconds, not only noise. Limiting glitches and leaving a time gap do not change it.

Making sure it is not the pipeline

Three checks on how the result was produced.

  • Shuffle the answers

    0.499 average

    Eye-state labels scrambled 20 times give a coin flip, as they should. The pipeline does not invent signal.

  • Leave a gap

    0.448 average

    Removing the 90 samples before each test block keeps the result near chance, so it is not leakage from neighbours.

  • Limit the glitches

    Rows 898, 10386, 11509, 13179

    Limits are learned from training rows only, separately for each block, so the test never shapes them.

How much history

Longer history looks better, but we did not pick a winner.

The 3, 9, 30 and 90-sample windows were set before looking. Ninety does best on average, but choosing it now would be fitting this one recording.

Next: test window lengths on new recordings before choosing.

Rerun it

python3 projects/eeg-eye-state/src/analyze.py writes results/metrics.json, and python3 website/build_results.py turns it into these charts. See the full report. Data: Roesler, O. (2013). EEG Eye State. UCI Machine Learning Repository, doi:10.24432/C57G7J.

What it does not show

One person and one short recording cannot show how this works for other people. The label is eyes open or closed, not attention, alertness or any condition.
