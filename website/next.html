Chapter 5 · Test, then go further

Where this goes next.

The loop does not stop at two studies and two apps. Each turn adds a study, a better design and a write-up others can check. Everything on this page is a plan, and it is labelled that way.

The road ahead

Each step builds on the last: data first, then people, then the two together.

  1. Now · done

    Two studies, two apps.

    The mouse and EEG studies, every result checked and rerunnable. Next Step and Helper Jobs working, with the four design rules built in.

    Shown: on this site, in the slides and in the phone tour.

  2. Next · with an ASDRP mentor

    Run the four data experiments.

    Brain-wave bands and drift in the EEG, using the data already here. Then timing and groups of brain cells in the full mouse recordings.

    Aim: a first paper, on why the EEG drifts, for a student research journal such as the Journal of Emerging Investigators, plus a poster.

  3. Then · with people

    Study the design with the people it is for.

    Co-design sessions with Pragnya volunteers and families, then a usability test of the four design rules. Consent first, and tasks written down before anyone takes part.

    Aim: a second paper, on the design rules, and better apps.

  4. Longer term · the joined-up question

    Does designing for state help people learn?

    Compare the energy-aware design with a fixed one, for the same person over several weeks. Then ask whether the brain findings predict who it helps most.

    Aim: one research direction, where neuroscience and human–computer interaction meet.

The next five experiments

One for each link in the loop, with the same steps as chapters 2 and 3.

  • 1 · Planned · tests chapter 2

    Does the timing matter?

    Does the mouse’s state at that exact moment help more than its state a little earlier or later? Needs the full mouse recordings.

  • 2 · Ready to run · tests chapter 3

    Brain-wave bands

    One kind of brain wave, called alpha, grows when eyes close. Does tracking it hold up over time better than the raw readings? Uses the recording we already have.

  • 3 · Planned · tests chapter 2

    Which brain cells change?

    Does state change every brain cell a little, or a few groups a lot? Groups are chosen before looking. Needs the full mouse recordings.

  • 4 · Started · tests chapter 3

    Why does it drift?

    How quickly does the eyes-closed pattern change, and can the model keep up if it keeps learning? The stretch-by-stretch scores and the broken-reading check are done.

  • 5 · Planned · closes the loop

    Do the apps help?

    Back to real life: do the four design rules make tasks easier to finish, for the people the apps are for? Needs consent, a task list written in advance and no personal data beyond what the test needs.

What each turn of the loop produces

Not just results: things other people can read, check and use.

  • Papers

    Write-ups others can check

    Every study ends in a paper with its code, data links and limits, aimed at student journals and science fairs.

  • Posters and talks

    Said out loud

    The same slides and charts as this site, for symposiums and science fairs.

  • Open tools

    Anyone can rerun it

    Every analysis reruns with one command, and the apps stay open source.

  • Better apps

    Back to real life

    Each design study changes the apps, and each change is tested again.

  • A design guide

    Rules others can use

    A short, plain guide to designing for brain state, built only from findings that held up.

  • Community

    Built with people, not for them

    Pragnya volunteers and families help decide what gets built and what gets tested.

The rule we keep: a plan stays labelled as a plan until it has code, saved results, a rerun record, its limits written down and a page like the two studies. No paper, poster or result is listed as done before it is.
