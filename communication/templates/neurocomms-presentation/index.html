Next Step · an early test app

Start your schoolwork, get back to it after a break, ask for what helps, and find friendly people. You decide each step.

For teenagers (about 13 to 17) whose brains work differently, for example autistic, ADHD or dyslexic.

An early test version. Every example is made up. It does not diagnose anything.

What goes wrong

Four times schoolwork gets stuck. The hard part is the moment, not you.

  • Starting

    You know roughly what to do, but can’t start

    Instructions spread over a handout, an email and a slide make the first step feel too big.

    Next Step: lists each task next to the line it came from, and marks what is unclear.

  • Coming back

    One interruption wipes out your place

    A bell, a message or a break, and the plan in your head is gone.

    Next Step: keeps a note of where you stopped, waiting for you on Home.

  • Saying what you need

    Explaining yourself, again and again

    Each new teacher needs the same explanation, and some people feel they must share a medical label first.

    Next Step: writes a polite request from a card in your own words. You never have to say why.

  • Finding your people

    Feeling alone, with nowhere safe online

    Big online chats bring strangers and pressure.

    Next Step: has small rooms for what you love. An adult checks every post first. No private messages.

Under all fourSome days you have less energy, so a plan made on a good day can be too much on a hard one. Next Step asks how much energy you have today, and shows less on tired days.

We read public Reddit and LinkedIn posts and published studies. Most posts are from adults, often about work, and we did not check ages. They show these problems are real for some people. They cannot show how common they are, or that this app helps. No young person has used Next Step yet. Paid sessions with teenagers come next.

The live rooms are a public test for adults: real people under made-up names, and nothing shows until a moderator approves it. Sign-in, age and parent checks are pretend, so it is not for real teenagers yet. The rooms are wiped every day, and the first visit after a quiet spell can take a minute to wake up.
