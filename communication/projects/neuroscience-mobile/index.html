The loop

From neurons to next steps

At Pragnya’s outings and in schoolwork, the same instruction can land one moment and be lost the next. Does what a brain is doing change what it takes in? Five short screens follow that question from real life, into the lab, and back out again.

Chapter 1 of 5 · Real life

Same words, different moment.

A step that is easy when someone is calm can be lost when they are tired, rushed or overloaded. Is that the brain itself? To find out, look inside a brain while its state changes.

Chapter 2 of 5 · Understand

A mouse’s state changes how its brain sees.

Mice watched pictures while thousands of brain cells were recorded. Guesser A saw only the picture. Guesser B also knew how alert and active the mouse was.

Found: knowing the mouse’s state made guesses 25–33% better, in all three mice.

Chapter 3 of 5 · Measure

Can sensors read state in a person?

A cap of 14 sensors on one person’s head for two minutes (EEG). Can a simple model tell when their eyes are closed?

Found: on one stretch, often. Across the recording it drifted to no better than guessing. Brain-wave bands helped, but still dipped below a coin flip.

Chapter 4 of 5 · Design

From findings to features.

State matters, but it cannot be read from outside. So the apps ask, not guess. They show less on a low-energy day, and make pausing and coming back easy.

Built into: Next Step, by Amishi Mehta, for schoolwork, and Helper Jobs, a family project, built for real jobs at Pragnya’s Sunday outings.
